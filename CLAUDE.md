## Design Context

### Users
This product serves technically curious students and self-learners studying Linux graphics, AMDGPU, and kernel-driver development. The interface should assume serious intent, high information density, and long-form reading sessions rather than casual browsing. The product is also bilingual, so layouts and component choices should remain robust across English and Chinese content.

### Brand Personality
The brand should feel technical, elite, and disciplined. It should communicate seriousness, credibility, and depth more than friendliness or playfulness. The emotional goal is to make learners feel that they are using a rigorous, trustworthy system that helps them level up toward real engineering work.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the current deep-space / AMD-lab visual direction. The core palette is a dark space-like foundation with AMD red-orange as the main accent, supported by precise typography, glass or panel layering, restrained glow, and code-first presentation. Light and dark themes should be equally polished rather than treating one as secondary.

Explicit anti-references:
- Do not make the UI look like a generic SaaS dashboard.
- Do not make it feel toy-like or gamified.
- Do not lean into flashy RGB gamer aesthetics or exaggerated cyberpunk effects.
- Do not use generic AI-generated gradient-card styling.

### Design Principles
1. Prioritize technical credibility over novelty. Interfaces should feel precise, intentional, and production-grade.
2. Support deep study. Favor strong hierarchy, readable density, stable layouts, and code/documentation-friendly presentation.
3. Preserve the AMD-lab atmosphere with restraint. Use the deep-space aesthetic and AMD accent color deliberately, not decoratively.
4. Treat bilingual clarity as a first-class requirement. Components must work cleanly for both English and Chinese text lengths.
5. Maintain accessibility as a baseline. Meet WCAG AA contrast standards and avoid visual choices that reduce legibility during long sessions.
